01 — Anatomy of Destruction

How knowledge dies

Across nearly 3,800 years, knowledge has been extinguished in remarkably consistent ways. Armed conflict — foreign invasion and civil war alike — accounts for nearly three in five of these records. But the pattern is not only one of malice: accidental fire alone has taken as many libraries as religious purges and state censorship combined.

So many books were thrown into the Tigris, the chroniclers wrote, that a horse could cross the river walking upon them.
— Traditional account of the fall of Baghdad, 1258. Widely repeated; unverifiable.

02 — Distribution Across Time

The rhythm of erasure

From ancient Mesopotamia to Gaza, Sudan and Ukraine today, no era has been free of the impulse to destroy what others preserved. The 20th century holds the most records of any century. The 21st is already within one of matching it, and it is not yet half over.

03 — Where It Happened

The fault lines

Destruction is not scattered evenly across the map. It gathers where empires met religions — the Fertile Crescent, the Balkans, the road between Constantinople and Baghdad. Every record in the catalogue, plotted.

What we cannot recover

The libraries recorded here are only the ones we know about. For every library whose destruction was witnessed, how many others vanished without a chronicler? The true count is, by definition, unknowable. And this record is not closed: entries dated 2025 and 2026 were added while it was being compiled.
